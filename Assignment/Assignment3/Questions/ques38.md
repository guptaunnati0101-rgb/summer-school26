Q38. LoRa & LPWAN — Long Range IoT
LPWAN (Low Power Wide Area Network) is a class of wireless networking technology designed to connect devices over distances of several kilometers while consuming very little power, at the cost of low data throughput — a trade-off ideal for sensors that send small readings infrequently (temperature, soil moisture, meter readings) rather than streaming media.
LoRa (Long Range) achieves this reach at low power through Chirp Spread Spectrum (CSS) modulation combined with a configurable Spreading Factor (SF). A higher spreading factor "stretches" each bit across a longer chirp signal, making it recoverable even far below the noise floor, at the cost of a lower data rate and longer airtime. SF7 gives faster, shorter-range transmission, while SF12 sacrifices speed for maximum range (10–15 km in open terrain) and superior penetration through obstacles.
Compared to Zigbee (short range, ~10–100 m, mesh topology, higher data rate, used for home automation) and Z-Wave (similar short range, proprietary, used mainly in smart home locks/lighting), LoRa trades data rate for dramatically longer range and lower power, making it the better fit for outdoor, sparse-node deployments.
Real-world example: Karnataka's "Krishi Doot" and similar LoRaWAN-based smart agriculture pilots in Indian states use LoRa sensor nodes across farmland to monitor soil moisture and automate irrigation over multi-kilometer ranges without cellular connectivity.
